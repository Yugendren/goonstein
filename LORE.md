# LORE.md — the conspiracy canon the hole is built from

Two lists.

**List A — FACTUAL.** Real, documented, JFK-era or earlier. Per DESIGN.md, this is the *only* material the
game may present as true. It's the load-bearing gag: the deeper you go the dumber it gets, but the museum
placards on the way down are all real, all sourced, and all from before 1965. Sources given.

**List B — MEME-ERA.** Modern internet lore. **Every item here is FICTION in the game.** We do not depict,
endorse or reproduce any of it. We take the *shape of the joke* and invent our own version with its own
name. No sources, because there's nothing to source — that's the point.

**Hard rules, restated because this is the file most likely to break them:**
- **No children.** Not as characters, props, text, silhouettes, implication or absence-that-points-at-them.
- **No living real people.** Archetypes only: The Prince, The Financier, The Scientist, The Guy Who Only
  Flew There Once.
- **Nothing about the real case.** No crimes, no victims, no client list, no dates, no death, no island name.
- **Only pre-1965 material may be presented as factual.**
- Everything in List B is renamed and reinvented before it enters the build.

# List A — FACTUAL (JFK-era or earlier)

These are for placards, tape recordings, filing-cabinet documents, the press-conference backdrop, loading
screens, and the running joke that the *boring true things* are stranger than anything downstairs.

### A1. Roswell (1947)
Debris found near Roswell, New Mexico was wreckage from **Project Mogul**, a classified array of high-altitude
balloons carrying acoustic sensors to detect Soviet nuclear tests.
*Myth gap:* the crashed-saucer-and-bodies story is not supported; the USAF's own 1997 explanation for the
"bodies" (anthropomorphic test dummies) is its account, not independently proven.
— https://www.af.mil/The-Roswell-Report/ · https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/html/tr/ADA326147/index.html

### A2. Project MKUltra (1953–~1973, CIA)
A real CIA umbrella programme funding covert research into drugs (especially LSD), hypnosis and behavioural
control, including tests on unwitting subjects.
*Myth gap:* no evidence it ever produced a controllable "programmed" person. The Director ordered most files
destroyed in 1973, so the full scope is permanently unknowable — which is the honest and creepier fact.
— https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/sites/default/files/hearings/95mkultra.pdf (Senate hearing, 3 Aug 1977)

### A3. Operation Northwoods (1962, Joint Chiefs of Staff)
The Joint Chiefs put in writing a proposal for staged attacks — fake hijackings, a sunk boat, faked assaults
on US targets — to manufacture a pretext for invading Cuba.
*Myth gap:* almost none. It really is the document people think it is. It was a **proposal**, rejected by the
Kennedy administration and never carried out. Declassified 1997.
— https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/CMC-60/joint-chiefs-pretexts-to-invade-Cuba-1962

### A4. Operation Paperclip (1945 onward, JIOA)
The US recruited ~1,600 German scientists after WWII, including former Nazi Party members, with officials
altering personnel records to get them past the ban on doing exactly that.
— https://www.history.com/articles/what-was-operation-paperclip

### A5. The grassy knoll (1963 / 1979 / 1982)
A real grassy rise in Dealey Plaza. The Warren Commission (1964) found no shot came from it; the House Select
Committee on Assassinations (1979) claimed a ~95% acoustic probability that one did, and concluded "probable
conspiracy."
*Myth gap — important:* the National Academy of Sciences' Ramsey Panel (1982) found the acoustic evidence was
recorded roughly a minute **after** the shooting. **Do not print the 95% figure as if it stands.** The honest
placard is: "a government committee said it, and then the physics said no."
— https://www.nationalacademies.org/publications/10264

### A6. Bohemian Grove (club founded 1872; grove 1899)
A private men's club campground in California with a theatrical "Cremation of Care" ceremony performed before
a large owl statue — an effigy burned to symbolise leaving your worries behind.
*The one genuinely significant thing that happened there:* on **13–14 September 1942**, the scientists of the
uranium committee — Lawrence, Oppenheimer, Compton, Conant — met at the Grove to plan the organisational
structure of what became the Manhattan Project.
*Myth gap:* the ritual is amateur theatre. The actual smoking gun is four physicists doing admin in a forest,
which is much funnier.
— https://ahf.nuclearmuseum.org/voices/oral-histories/harold-fidlers-interview/

### A7. Tesla's papers (1943)
After Tesla's death, the federal Office of Alien Property Custodian seized his papers. MIT engineer **John G.
Trump** reviewed them and concluded the last ~15 years of material was "primarily of a speculative,
philosophical, and promotional character," with nothing new and workable in it.
*Myth gap:* no death ray was found, because there wasn't one. The genuinely odd part is the seizure itself.
The FBI has since released 250+ pages of its Tesla file under FOIA.
— https://www.history.com/articles/nikola-tesla-files-declassified-fbi

### A8. Project Blue Book (1952–1969, USAF)
The Air Force's public UFO investigation logged ~**12,618** reports, of which ~**701 (6%)** were filed
"unidentified." The Condon Report (Jan 1969) concluded further study wasn't scientifically warranted and Blue
Book closed that December.
— https://www.britannica.com/topic/Project-Blue-Book

### A9. The Philadelphia Experiment — **a claim, not an event** (alleged 1943)
The story that USS *Eldridge* was made invisible and teleported traces entirely to letters sent in **1955** by
one "Carlos Allende" (Carl Allen) to a UFO writer. The Navy investigated and found nothing; the ship's deck
logs place it elsewhere on the claimed date.
*The mundane kernel:* **degaussing** — really demagnetising ships' hulls so magnetic mines couldn't see them.
"Invisible" to a sensor, not to the eye.
— https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/nhhc/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/p/philadelphia-experiment/philadelphia-experiment-onr-info-sheet.html

### A10. Area 51 / Groom Lake (site selected April 1955)
Chosen by CIA and Lockheed staff as a secret flight-test site for the U-2 spy plane; first U-2 flight
4 August 1955. The CIA's internal U-2 history, released in fuller form in **2013**, was the first officially
acknowledged government document naming the base.
*The real link to UFOs:* high-altitude U-2 and A-12 flights genuinely account for a chunk of 1950s–60s
sighting reports, by the Air Force's own admission.
— https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB434/

### A11. Operation Mockingbird — **be careful with this one**
What is documented: the **Church Committee** (1976) found roughly **50 American journalists** with secret
relationships with the CIA, plus a larger foreign network used for covert propaganda placement.
*Myth gap:* the codename "Mockingbird" and the idea of a unified command structure controlling the US press
come from later journalism, not from the Committee's findings. Placard the finding, not the name.
— https://www.intelligence.senate.gov (Church Committee reports)

### A12. Tuskegee Syphilis Study (1932–1972, US Public Health Service)
~600 Black men in Macon County, Alabama were enrolled in a study that deliberately withheld effective
treatment — including penicillin once it existed — to observe untreated disease, without informed consent.
*Note:* the men were **not** infected by the study; the abuse was the withholding and the deception.
Presidential apology, 1997. Real, documented, and not a joke — use as a placard, never as a gag.
— https://www.cdc.gov/tuskegee/about/index.html

### A13. Project Sunshine / human radiation experiments (1944–1974, AEC & DoD)
Cold War programmes involving radiation exposure of human subjects, often without consent; Project Sunshine
covertly collected human tissue worldwide to measure strontium-90 fallout. Documented by the **Advisory
Committee on Human Radiation Experiments** (1994–95, ~925-page report).
*Handle like A12: real institutional harm. Placard, not punchline.*
— https://ehss.energy.gov/ohre/roadmap/achre/summary.html

### A14. Operation Sea-Spray (September 1950, US Navy)
The Navy sprayed bacteria over San Francisco Bay to model a biological attack on a city.
— https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/1950-us-released-bioweapon-san-francisco-180955819/

### A15. Project A119 (1958, USAF)
A classified study, "A Study of Lunar Research Flights," assessed detonating a nuclear device on the Moon for
Cold War prestige. Cancelled January 1959; revealed in 2000 by the physicist who led it.
— https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_A119

### A16. Project Horizon (1959, US Army)
A feasibility study for a **twelve-man military outpost on the Moon** by 1966, at roughly $6 billion.
Rejected when space work went to civilian NASA.
— http://www.astronautix.com/p/projecthorizon.html

### A17. Operation Highjump (1946–47, US Navy)
A genuinely enormous Antarctic expedition — ~4,700 personnel under Admiral Byrd — to establish a postwar US
presence and logistics capability.
*Myth gap:* the "secret battle with a Nazi UFO base" story has **no basis whatsoever** in the operational
record. This is the boring true event that Antarctica lore inflates. Perfect placard for the ice floor.
— https://historynet.com/operation-highjump-1946-47-expedition-to-explore-antarctica-from-the-air/

### A18. Acoustic Kitty (CIA, ~1960s, cancelled 1967)
The CIA surgically implanted a cat with listening and transmitting equipment to use as a mobile bug. A
declassified memo records the programme and its cancellation as impractical.
*The single best free joke in the entire factual list. Put the cat on the wall.*
— https://unredacted.com/2010/03/05/document-friday-acoustic-kitty/

### A19. The Hollow Nickel case (1953, FBI)
A Brooklyn paperboy was given a hollowed-out nickel containing microfilm; the FBI traced it to a Soviet
illegal, convicted in 1957. Real spy-craft, real hollow coin.
— https://www.fbi.gov/history/cases-and-criminals/hollow-nickel-rudolph-abel

### A20. The U-2 incident (1 May 1960)
A CIA pilot was shot down over the USSR; the US issued a cover story about a stray weather plane, which fell
apart when the Soviets produced the pilot alive.
— https://history.state.gov/milestones/1953-1960/u2-incident

### A21. Operation Ajax (August 1953, CIA + SIS)
The CIA, with British intelligence, covertly organised the overthrow of Iran's prime minister. The CIA
formally acknowledged it in 2013 with a declassified internal history.
— https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB28/

### A22. Bay of Pigs (April 1961, CIA)
A CIA-organised, funded and trained invasion of Cuba by exiles, which collapsed in three days. Openly
acknowledged history rather than a theory.
— https://www.britannica.com/event/Bay-of-Pigs-invasion

### ⚠ A23. Operation Gladio — **outside the cutoff, do not use as "factual"**
NATO-linked "stay-behind" networks. Founding period is mid/late 1950s, but everything that makes it famous —
the terrorism allegations (1969–80) and the disclosure (1990) — is decades past the JFK-era line. **Fails the
DESIGN.md rule.** Listed here only so nobody puts it on a placard by accident.

**Placard discipline:** A12 and A13 are real harms to real people. They can appear as sourced wall text in the
"actually true" exhibit; they must never be a joke, a loot item or an enemy.

# List B — MEME-ERA (FICTION IN THE GAME)

For each: **the shape of the joke** (what the punchline structurally *is*), then **how it becomes a room, an
enemy or a loot item**. All in-game names are placeholders — rename before the build.

### B1. The Striped Pavilion ("the temple")
**Shape:** a rich eccentric builds one conspicuously weird building and refuses to explain it, so everyone
explains it for him. Belongs to the real folly genre — Coral Castle, the Winchester Mystery House, garden
follies, the Ryugyong Hotel: monomaniacal overcommitment where the *why* becomes more famous than the thing.
**Game:** the first landmark and the descent's front door. **Room:** a striped cube whose interior is
absurdly, disappointingly ordinary — a piano, a rug, a bad ceiling mural of the constellations. **Loot:** the
building's own **planning application**, which describes a modest octagonal music room, held next to the
thirty-foot cube it authorised. **The door itself** is a two-player carry (DESIGN.md lists it as evidence).

### B2. The tunnels
**Shape:** "there is something under there" applied to any hole. The escalation ladder — cistern, then
bunker, then city, then civilisation.
**Game:** **Floor 2 is a cistern.** Genuinely just a cistern. The players' disappointment is the joke, and the
door at the far end of it is how you keep going.

### B3. The submarine
**Shape:** two jokes in one coat — the billionaire buying a cramped dangerous vehicle as a toy, and the
WWII submarine pen (real, mundane military concrete) being so cinematic that fiction can't stop using it as
an evil lair.
**Game:** **Room:** a flooded pen far too large for the island above it. **Loot:** a **monogrammed personal
submersible** that four players can push, float, capsize and lose. **Enemy:** nothing — the sub is the hazard.

### B4. The catchphrase
**Shape:** a fixed absurd phrase bolted onto the end of any unrelated sentence, deadpan, regardless of
context. The comedy is entirely timing and incongruity; it works as a shibboleth because saying it correctly
proves fluency, not belief.
**Game:** **we take the mechanic, never the phrase.** We invent our own four-word non-sequitur, and NPCs,
radio broadcasts and the press-conference journalists append it to unrelated sentences at the worst possible
moment. **Never reference the real one. It doesn't exist in this world.**

### B5. The lizards
**Shape:** a blurry frame or a compression artefact treated as forensic proof of an ancient shapeshifting
bloodline — wildly disproportionate inference from a bad JPEG.
**⚠ Handle:** the "hidden bloodline secretly rules the world" structure is lifted from classic antisemitic
hoax literature, with "reptilian" doing coded work. **We do not use that structure at all** — no secret
bloodline, no hidden rulers, no shapeshifting-as-disguise.
**Our version:** the island really did import twenty-five iguanas in 2002 (see ISLAND.md). So the lizards
are just **lizards**, they are **staff**, and they are **on a break**. **Room:** the Reptile Lounge — folding
chairs, a rota, a kettle. **Enemy:** iguanas who will fight you *only* if you take their chairs. **Loot:**
the shift rota. It's a workplace-comedy floor, not a bloodline floor.

### B6. The drug
**Shape:** ⚠ the modern "harvesting" conspiracy is a repackaged blood libel and **we discard it completely** —
frame, mechanics and vocabulary. What we keep is where the *joke* version actually came from: a 1971 gonzo
novel's invented, wildly over-specific fake pharmacology, played for absurdist comedy. The real chemical is a
boring oxidation product of adrenaline that does nothing.
**Game:** invent a fictional substance from scratch with an idiotically specific fake production process
involving something harmless and stupid (limes, printer toner, the pool filter). **Loot:** a fragile glowing
vial. **Room:** a lab where the substance is being extracted, at enormous expense, from tinned fruit.

### B7. The black book
**Shape:** a pure MacGuffin — a ledger of names whose *existence* threatens the powerful, whose contents are
never shown. Withholding the names is the trope. Ripe for anticlimax.
**Game:** **Loot:** loose pages, carried separately, individually fragile, so four players must each carry
some and lose some. **The anticlimax:** the recovered pages are a **catering order**, a **wine inventory**,
and a list of everyone who owes for the boat fuel. The press conference shrugs harder than usual.

### B8. The painting
**Shape:** a portrait hung somewhere mundane whose subject is one degree past normal — too knowing, eyes that
track you. Works because real institutions really do hang portraits of founders, so the parody only has to
push slightly. Funnier when acknowledged casually than when explained.
**Game:** **Loot:** the flagship fragile object — huge, gilt-framed, two-player carry, doesn't fit through
doors, must go in the boat. **Enemy:** it isn't one, but its eyes track the nearest player, and no NPC will
comment on this. **Gag:** the same portrait reappears one floor down, larger.

### B9. Flat earth / the ice wall
**Shape:** elaborate pseudo-scientific apparatus deployed with total sincerity to defend an obviously false
premise, and then the apparatus honestly reports the wrong answer.
**Game:** **Room:** the edge of the map, rendered as an actual retaining wall with a maintenance gantry and a
works notice. **Enemy:** surveyors defending their instrument. **Loot:** a very expensive gyroscope whose
readout keeps confirming the thing its owners don't want confirmed.

### B10. Birds aren't real
**Shape:** the cleanest template on this list — a conspiracy movement performed with full bureaucratic
sincerity (recruiters, merch, press-release cadence) by people who openly say it's a bit. The commitment is
the punchline.
**Game:** **Room:** a charging depot — rows of perches, docking cradles, a wall of firmware update schedules.
**Enemy:** seagulls that dock to recharge and must be unplugged. **Loot:** a **pigeon with a service hatch**,
fragile, and it squawks when you drop it.

### B11. The simulation
**Shape:** two registers — a real philosophy argument, and the meme layer where any coincidence or video
artefact is posted deadpan as leaked source code. Plus the NPC bit: flattening a person into scripted
background behaviour.
**Game:** the second-deepest floor. **Room:** a corridor that repeats with one object moved each pass.
**Enemy:** NPCs on a five-second loop who are polite and completely unbothered by you. **Loot:** a piece of
the floor's own geometry, which you can carry, and which leaves a hole.

### B12. The Denver airport model
**Shape:** the most useful structural lesson here. Mundane civic art — murals, a blue horse, a dedication
capstone with an irresistible committee name — over-read into a nerve centre, and then **the accused
institution leans all the way in and sells merch about it.**
**Game:** not a floor — a **tone**. Every faction the players meet already knows the rumours about itself and
has a gift shop. **Loot:** the compound's own **official conspiracy-tour audio guide**, sold at reception.

### B13. The bunker ladder
**Shape:** real hardened concrete (dull civil-defence engineering) narrated as a multi-level facility, with
numbered sublevels that get sillier the deeper the list goes. Adjacent: the billionaire doomsday bunker,
which is real behaviour and openly mocked.
**Game:** the **spine of the whole descent** — a lift panel whose buttons run out of sensible labels around
floor 4 and start using symbols, then adjectives.

### B14. The elite gathering
**Shape:** genuinely closed-door meetings of genuinely powerful people, plus robes and an owl, which pushes
it into camp. Just enough real texture to feel plausible.
**Game:** **Room:** a forest clearing that is somehow underground. **Enemy:** robed attendees who are
extremely embarrassed to be seen and will flee rather than fight. **Loot:** the giant owl. It is enormous.
Four players. It does not fit in the boat, and that is the point of the floor.

### B15. Weather control
**Shape:** a real, boring antenna field blamed reflexively for all weather. Now a one-word punchline.
**Game:** **Room:** a rooftop antenna array with a single dial reading DRIZZLE / BRISK / BIBLICAL. **Hazard:**
players fighting over the dial. **Loot:** the dial.

### B16. Contrails
**Shape:** paranoid pattern-recognition applied to a mundane thing everyone can look up and check.
**Game:** a light touch — background only. Aircraft cross the dusk sky in a neat crosshatch grid, and the
skybox is *visibly* on a schedule.

### B17. Antarctica / hollow earth
**Shape:** the edge of the map is suspicious *because* it's cold, empty and treaty-regulated. Stacks with
B9's ice wall and with A17's very real, very boring expedition.
**Game:** **Room:** a cold floor with a mineshaft going further down and a 1940s expedition placard on the
wall — a true one (A17). **Enemy:** penguins with clipboards. **Loot:** a flag nobody planted.

### B18. The cryptids
**Shape:** the friendliest entry — a monster fully absorbed by the local tourism board. Statues, festivals,
plush toys. The threat has become a civic mascot.
**Game:** the **relief valve** between hard floors. **Room:** a gift shop staffed by the cryptid. **Enemy:**
none; it wants a photo with you. **Loot:** a souvenir plush of itself, which sells at the press conference
for more than any real evidence. *This is the game's thesis in one item.*

### B19. Mandela effect
**Shape:** confidently misremembered detail treated as proof of a timeline split.
**Game:** every object in the compound has its logo spelled *slightly* wrong, and it changes between runs. No
achievement, no acknowledgement. Never mentioned.

### B20. The place that doesn't exist
**Shape:** the deadpan absurdist bit — a real place declared fictional, delivered with mock rigour. Its whole
family (Bielefeld, Finland, Australia) was self-parody from day one, which makes it the safest well on this
list.
**Game:** **Room:** a records office maintaining the paperwork that proves a place isn't there. **Enemy:**
clerks. **Loot:** the deed to nowhere.

### B21. Free energy
**Shape:** "they suppressed him," martyr-genius framing applied to any unexplained sparking thing. Pairs with
A7, which is the true and much sadder version.
**Game:** **Room:** a workshop of enormous coils that draw power *from the mains*, visibly, via an extension
lead. **Loot:** the extension lead.

### B22. Tartaria / mud flood
**Shape:** ornate old architecture reframed as the remnant of an erased civilisation, buried by a flood.
Underneath it is really just "why is new stuff ugly."
**Game:** **Room:** a grand marble hall buried to the window line in dried mud, with a modern plastic office
partition installed inside it.

### B23. The portal
**Shape:** every time the big machine is switched on, someone declares a portal has opened. Fully ironic — a
running joke, not a belief.
**Game:** the bottom-adjacent floor. **Room:** a large machine with a warning sign, a switch and a queue.
**Enemy:** whatever came through, which is a middle-manager.

### B24. The filing cabinet
**Shape:** DESIGN.md's own optional punchline, and the correct ending. After every escalation, the deepest
secret is administrative.
**Game:** **Floor 10.** A room containing one filing cabinet. It is locked. The key is on the boat.

**Interstitials.** Drop the **cryptid gift shop** (B18) between any two floors as a breather — it sells a

**Where the true stuff lives.** List A is the *museum layer*: placards, tapes and cabinet documents scattered
across floors 0–4, where the setting is still pretending to be serious. The gag is that a player who reads
every placard on the way down learns only true, sourced, pre-1965 history, and then arrives at a lizard on a
tea break. Put **Acoustic Kitty** (A18) on floor 6's wall, **Highjump** (A17) on floor 9's, **Northwoods**
(A3) in the records office, and **Tesla's papers** (A7) next to the extension lead (B21).

# Handling notes

Things that will bite us if we're careless.

1. **The lizard floor.** DESIGN.md calls it "the lizard lounge." Keep the name if you like it, but **the
   reptilian trope's underlying structure — a hidden bloodline secretly ruling the world — is recycled
   antisemitic hoax material.** We take literal lizards on a tea break and nothing else. No bloodline, no
   secret rulers, no shapeshifting disguise. The island's real imported iguanas give us a clean, funny,
   totally unrelated origin. Use it.
2. **The drug floor.** The modern "harvesting" conspiracy is a repackaged blood libel. **Discard the entire
   frame**, including its vocabulary. Keep only the 1971-novel joke shape: invented, over-specific, absurd
   pharmacology. Our substance is made from tinned fruit and has its own made-up name.
3. **The catchphrase.** We take only the *mechanic* — a fixed non-sequitur bolted onto unrelated sentences.
   We invent our own phrase. The real one is never spoken, printed, referenced or alluded to, and the events
   behind it do not exist in this world.
4. **A12 and A13** (Tuskegee, the radiation experiments) are real harm to real people. Placards only. Never a
   gag, a loot item, an enemy or a room.
5. **A23 (Gladio)** breaks the pre-1965 rule. Excluded from the factual layer.
6. **The Denver lesson is the game's whole tone.** The most useful thing in List B isn't a floor, it's the
   posture: the institution being accused already knows, and has merch. That's what makes a conspiracy
   comedy instead of a conspiracy. Every faction downstairs should be *tired of being a mystery.*
7. **Rename everything in List B before it enters the build.** These are joke shapes, not content. If a
   player can identify which real meme a room is "doing," we've done it too literally.
